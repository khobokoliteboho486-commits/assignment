Maletsunyane Falls

A 192-metre single-drop waterfall near Semonkong, one of the highest in Southern Africa. Guided hikes and abseiling available.

Katse Dam

Africa's highest dam at 185 metres, set in the Maluti Mountains. Guided tours reveal the engineering behind the water project.

Adventure

Sani Pass

A legendary mountain pass climbing over 1,300 metres through the Drakensberg escarpment into Lesotho. 4x4 required.

Nature

Sehlabathebe National Park

Lesotho's oldest protected area and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, home to rare Afro-Alpine species and ancient rock art.

Heritage

Thaba Bosiu

The sacred mountain fortress of King Moshoeshoe I — the birthplace of the Basotho nation and a national monument.

Adventure

Afriski Mountain Resort

Ski and snowboard at 3,222 metres in winter, or hike and mountain bike through the Maluti Mountains in summer.

Lesotho's historic mission town, home to the Morija Museum & Archives and the country's oldest church, founded in 1833.

Before You Go

Travel Information

Practical notes for planning a visit. Please verify current requirements with official sources before travelling.

Lesotho is reachable by road from South Africa, with border crossings at Maseru Bridge, Maputsoe, Caledonspoort, Van Roosens Nek, and Qacha's Nek. The mountain kingdom also has an international airport at Moshoeshoe I International Airport near Maseru. Check current entry requirements before travelling.

Lesotho has distinct seasons. Summer (November–February) brings warm days and afternoon thunderstorms. Autumn (March–May) offers clear skies and golden highland grasses. Winter (June–August) brings snow to the high peaks and ski season at Afriski. Spring (September–October) is wildflower season. Each season has its own appeal — check local forecasts and pack accordingly.

Much of Lesotho lies above 2,000 metres, and passes such as Sani reach over 2,800 metres. Some travellers may feel the effects of altitude. Take your time, stay hydrated, and allow a day or two to acclimatise before strenuous hikes. Consult a travel health professional before your trip.

The currency is the Loti (plural Maloti), which is pegged 1:1 to the South African Rand. Rand is widely accepted. ATMs are available in Maseru and larger towns, but rural areas may be cash-only. Carry sufficient cash for mountain journeys and inform your bank before travelling.
